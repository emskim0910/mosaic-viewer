MOSAIC · Rev 5

A propellant vessel
printed as one part.

MOSAIC is a low-cost cold-gas thruster for CubeSat-class small satellites. Its pressure vessel, isogrid skin, internal webs and port bosses are a single additively manufactured AlSi10Mg print: no welds, no joined panels. The Rev 5 print is the vessel; nozzle and feed integration are in work.

Overview

Monolithic, Optimized & Scalable Additively Integrated Cold-Gas thruster.

MOSAIC targets a 1U form factor and addresses the mass penalty and leak risk of conventional CubeSat propulsion by additively manufacturing the pressure vessel, feed manifolds and nozzle as a single AlSi10Mg structure. The Rev 5 vessel is printed and analyzed; nozzle and feed integration are the next step.

01

Low cost

$3K per unit against a $100K+ industry standard. Architectural simplification that prioritizes reliability and market access for university groups, independent labs and early-stage operators.

02

Monolithic integration

The AlSi10Mg pressure vessel is designed to double as the CubeSat's load-bearing chassis, printed by LPBF/SLM; launch-load cases have not yet been run. Deadweight and leak points are designed out rather than sealed up.

03

Scalability

Standardized internal manifolds scale across 1U, 2U and 3U configurations without redesign.

The vessel · Rev 5, October 2026

Everything a tank needs, grown in one build.

The exploded view above separates regions of a single printed body so you can see how it is put together. In the real part there is nothing to put together.

01

Flat skins

1.5 mm skin walls and 1.8 mm floor and roof caps close a nominal 96 × 86 × 96 mm box. The skin carries pressure as a flat panel; the structure around it stops it bulging.

02

External isogrid

600 triangular pockets across the four side faces: 10.2 mm triangles, ribs 1.2 × 2.5 mm, R1 fillets, on the outside of the skin where they stiffen it without taking internal volume.

03

Internal web core

A 5 × 5 lattice of 1.2 mm webs ties every wall to its opposite and divides the volume into cells, connected to one another through reinforced interconnect openings.

04

Transducer well and port bosses

A well for the pressure transducer sits inside the tank volume rather than outside the envelope. An outlet boss (1/4-28) and a fill boss (10-32) are formed on the floor, each finished with a threaded insert.

Technology evolution strategy

Cold gas first, then warm gas, then water.

Phase 1 · short term

Drag compensation and deorbiting

A drag-compensation and deorbiting service for VLEO CubeSats and constellations, where electric propulsion is expensive overkill.

Phase 2 · medium term

Warm-gas thruster

A cartridge heat exchanger upstream of the nozzle raises Isp beyond cold-gas limits.

Phase 3 · long term

Water resistojet

Steam-based thrust benchmarked against Pale Blue's non-ITAR PBR-20. Water propellant removes pressure-storage concerns and improves Isp further.
