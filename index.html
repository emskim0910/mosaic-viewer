Monolithic, Optimized & Scalable Additively Integrated Cold-Gas (MOSAIC) Thruster

A low-cost, monolithically integrated cold gas thruster (CGT) for CubeSat-class small satellites, targeting a 1U form factor. The MOSAIC thruster addresses the mass penalty and leak risks of conventional propulsion solutions by additively manufacturing the pressure vessel, feed manifolds, and nozzle as a single AlSi10Mg structure.

Current state-of-the-art CubeSat propulsion systems cost more than US$100,000 per unit, pricing out university research groups, independent labs, and early-stage commercial operators. At US$3,000 per unit, MOSAIC achieves a 97% reduction in unit cost through architectural simplification that prioritizes reliability and market accessibility. Ranked 3rd among 95 key shortfalls identified by NASA's technology taxonomy, small spacecraft propulsion is a critical and underserved capability gap.

NASA Space Grant Brown University Open Research

Three Differentiators

  • Low cost: $3K per unit vs $100K+ industry standard (97% reduction)
  • Monolithic integration: AlSi10Mg pressure vessel doubles as the CubeSat's load-bearing chassis via LPBF/SLM, eliminating deadweight and leak points
  • Scalability: standardized internal manifolds scale across 1U, 2U and 3U without redesign

Development Roadmap

Development Phases

  • I
    CAD: 1U, 2U & 3U Configurations
    Monolithic model integrating pressure vessel, internal manifolds, and nozzle in a single continuous print. Geometry designed to scale for 2U and 3U configurations.
    Status · Oct 2026: vessel Rev5 complete (the model in the viewer); nozzle and feed integration in work.
  • II
    FEA & Topology Optimization
    Remove surplus material from non-load-bearing regions while maintaining a 2× safety factor under simulated launch loads up to 10G.
    Status · Oct 2026: pressure sizing in progress across five vessel revisions (wall, junction, corner and hole models); SolidWorks checks on Rev4 and Rev5; launch loads not yet run.
  • III
    CFD: Internal Manifold Optimization
    Nonlinear CFD to optimize curvilinear manifolds, minimize stagnation points, and characterize the discharge coefficient of the feed path.
  • IV
    AM Fabrication & COTS Integration
    Additive manufacture the prototype and integrate all commercial subsystems into the completed assembly.
  • V
    Leak Testing & Cold-Flow Testing
    Validate the fluid and feed system performance with a nontoxic inert fluid.
  • VI
    Data Analysis & Presentation
    Compile findings from leak and cold-flow tests and summarize lessons learned.

Technology Evolution Strategy

Phase 1: Short-term
Drag-Compensation & Deorbiting
MOSAIC is a drag-compensation and deorbiting service for VLEO CubeSats and constellations, in which electrical propulsion systems are expensive overkill.
Phase 2: Medium-term
Warm-Gas Thruster
Add a cartridge heat exchanger upstream of the nozzle to raise Isp beyond cold-gas limits.
Phase 3: Long-term
Water Resistojet
Transition to steam-based thrust benchmarked against Pale Blue's non-ITAR PBR-20. Water propellant eliminates pressure storage concerns and improves Isp further.

About

Spacecraft propulsion hardware, from CAD to cold‑flow test.

I'm Ethan M. Kim, a mechanical engineering student at Brown University and principal investigator on MOSAIC — a monolithically integrated, additively manufactured cold-gas thruster for CubeSat-class satellites, supported by a NASA Space Grant and a Brown University Engineering Grant.

The work runs the full engineering cycle rather than a single slice of it: parametric CAD of a pressure vessel that doubles as the satellite's load-bearing chassis, FEA and topology optimization against launch loads, nonlinear CFD on the internal feed manifolds, design for laser powder-bed fusion in AlSi10Mg, and validation by leak and cold-flow testing. The driving constraint is cost: current CubeSat propulsion runs upward of US$100,000 per unit, which prices out exactly the university labs and early-stage operators most likely to fly it.

I also build the analysis tooling when what's available doesn't fit. Both interactive pieces on this site — the WebGL CAD viewer above and the isentropic nozzle flow simulator below — are browser applications written from scratch, with no build step; the simulator has no dependencies at all.

MOSAIC is advised by Rick D. Fleeter, Ph.D. (Adjunct Associate Professor, Brown University; former President, AeroAstro) with Jeanue Chung (Founder & President, LUNR Aerospace Corporation) as subject-matter expert.

Capabilities

What this work demanded

Each item below is exercised somewhere in the MOSAIC program or its supporting tooling — not a generic skills list.

Design & Structural Analysis

  • Parametric CAD — monolithic vessel, manifolds and nozzle as one continuous body; 1U, 2U and 3U configurations
  • FEA & topology optimization — material removed from non-load-bearing regions at a 2× safety factor under 10G launch loads
  • Pressure vessel design — 500 psig MEOP with 800 psig FEA capability; flat skins, external isogrid and an internal web core, doing double duty as the CubeSat chassis

Fluids & Thermodynamics

  • Nonlinear CFD — curvilinear manifold optimization, stagnation-point suppression, feed-path discharge coefficient
  • Compressible nozzle flow — quasi-1D isentropic analysis of a converging–diverging nozzle, 3–5:1 area ratio
  • Performance modelling — thrust, Isp, mass flow and flow-regime classification across back pressures

Manufacturing & Materials

  • DfAM / LPBF · SLM — AlSi10Mg on an EOS M290; floor-down build with a self-supporting web core and 45° roof chamfers, no internal supports
  • Post-processing — stress relief at 300 °C, <0.1% porosity target; HIP under review against the as-built allowables
  • Precision finishing — EDM / laser-drilled 0.3–0.5 mm throat, electropolished to Ra <3.2 µm to suppress crack initiation

Systems, Test & Software

  • Feed-system integration — regulator, solenoid and filter train, with a blowdown variant traded; COTS selection across transducer, regulator, valve, fittings, MCU and PC/104 bus
  • Validation — leak testing and inert-fluid cold-flow characterization
  • Engineering tooling — WebGL/Three.js CAD visualization and a dependency-free numerical flow simulator, both built in-house

02 Tooling

Isentropic Nozzle Flow Simulator

Interactive quasi-1D compressible flow solver · zero dependencies

A single-file simulation of quasi-1D isentropic flow through the MOSAIC converging–diverging nozzle, written without libraries, build step or network access. It renders a Mach contour with animated streamlines, live axial profiles of M, P/P₀, T/T₀ and ρ/ρ₀, and running performance stats — thrust, Isp, ṁ and exit Mach — while classifying the flow regime. An Optimize Shape routine sweeps area ratio ε and length L/L₁₅° to maximize thrust at the current back pressure.
